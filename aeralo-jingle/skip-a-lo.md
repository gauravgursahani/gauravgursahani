# Aeralo — "Skip-a-lo"

**Insight:** travel makes us children again, until fear makes us adults.
**Answer:** let your phone be the grown-up, so you can be the kid.
**Payoff line:** **Get lost. Stay found.**
**Tagline (sung, inside the song):** **Wherever you go… Aeralo.**
**Pronunciation:** **AIR-a-lo**

---

## The payoff

The payoff has to answer the insight's tension (*I want to wander, but I'm scared of getting lost*) and leave the brand behind in your head. It does that in three layers that all land in the last ten seconds.

1. **Story: the roles swap.** A grown-up traveller is dragged around the city by her own inner child, a small girl who is a younger version of her. The adult keeps hesitating. At the climax she stops and asks, *"Where are we?"* One glance at her phone shows a blue dot and full signal. She puts the phone away, **grabs the kid's hand and leads.** The phone has become the grown-up, so she can be the kid.
2. **Words: "Get lost. Stay found."** Four words that resolve the tension. It's the only spoken line in the whole film, delivered in a calm adult voice after a minute of nasal playground chant.
3. **Brand: the tagline is a lyric, not an add-on.** The last line of the song is *"Wherever-you-go-a-lo… AIR-a-LO!"* The brand name is the final, loudest beat of the rhyme the audience has been chanting all along, so they sing the brand themselves.

## Brand assets this creates

| Asset | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **The "-a-lo" language** | A nonsense ending the brand owns | Social posts ("coffee-a-lo", "beach-a-lo"), app copy ("You're online-a-lo!") |
| **Sonic logo** | Three rope slaps, then nasal *"AIR-a-lo"* in the same three-beat rhythm | Ad endings, app open, the eSIM-connected sound |
| **The skipping blue dot** | A location dot that bounces like a skipping kid | Logo animation, the "connected" screen, the loading state |
| **Payoff line** | *Get lost. Stay found.* | Print, out-of-home, airport ads |
| **Tagline as lyric** | *Wherever you go… Aeralo* | Inside every version of the song |
| **The inner-child device** | A kid version of the traveller | A campaign series: new traveller, new inner child, new cities |

## Notes
- **Casting the kid:** she must look like a real younger version of the adult. That's the emotional core.
- **The silence matters:** the bridge has to be properly quiet, so the "Found-a-lo!" rope slap feels like relief.
- **Don't show the app interface at length.** One glance at the phone is enough. The product is the confidence it gives, not the screen.
- **Other languages:** "-a-lo" is nonsense, so it works in any language. Translate the verbs (skip, hop) and keep the endings.
