# Aeralo — Concepts from "Childlike Wonder"

**Insight:** travel makes us children again, until fear makes us adults. Reliable data in your pocket lets the kid in you off the leash.
**Form:** children's rhymes (skipping rhymes, counting games, camp chants). These are some of the stickiest verse forms people know, and they echo the baby-talk quality of "Honey Bunny" and the playground sing-song of Close-Up.
**Tagline:** *Wherever you go, Aeralo.*
**Pronunciation:** **AIR-a-lo**

---

## Concept A: "Skip-a-lo" (a skipping rhyme)

Kids add nonsense endings to everything. Here, every word gets an "-a-lo" ending, so the brand name sounds like part of a made-up playground language. *SKIP-a-lo, HOP-a-lo, AIR-a-lo* all share the same bouncy three-beat rhythm.

```
Skip-a-lo, hop-a-lo,
Up-the-hill-and-down-a-lo,
Left-a-lo, right-a-lo,
Lost-a-lo… found-a-lo…
AIR-a-LO!

Wherever you go… Aeralo.
```

- **Delivery:** a nasal kid's voice in a skipping-rope rhythm. The slap of the rope is the beat.
- **Film idea:** grown-ups abroad behaving like kids. They skip along tram lines in Lisbon, roll down a Scottish hill, chase pigeons in Venice, and take random turns down Tokyo backstreets. Nobody checks their phone. It stays in their pocket.
- **The key line:** *"Lost-a-lo… found-a-lo"* is the insight in two words: get lost, and you'll still be found.
- **Why it's sticky:** it's nonsense, the rhythm is relentless, and "-a-lo" gets into your head and spreads to other words. People will start saying "coffee-a-lo".

---

## Concept B: "Ready or Not" (hide-and-seek)

The countdown before a game of hide-and-seek, with the city as the playground.

```
(nasal kid, eyes covered)
One… two… three… four… five…
six… seven… eight… nine… TEN!

Ready or not —
here I GO…
AIR-a-LO!
```

- **Film idea:** a traveller presses their face to a hotel window with eyes covered, counting. On "ten" they burst out of the door and disappear into Marrakech, Mexico City or Seoul.
- **Super:** *Get lost. You'll always be found.* Then: *Wherever you go, Aeralo.*
- **Why it's sticky:** everyone on earth knows the countdown. "Here I go… Aeralo" rhymes. It also works well as a series, with a new city for each round.

---

## Concept C: "Don't Know!" (a call-and-response camp chant)

A leader calls and the group answers, the way kids chant on a school trip. Not knowing becomes the fun part.

```
(leader)  Where are we?
(all)     DON'T KNOW!
(leader)  Where we going?
(all)     DON'T KNOW!
(leader)  Are we lost?
(all)     Oh no, no, no —
          we're AIR-a-LO!
```

- **The twist:** "Oh no" is normally the panic line. Here, "no, no, no" means *we're not lost*.
- **Film idea:** a group of grown-up friends march through a foreign night market, a desert or a mountain village chanting it like a scout troop, joyfully clueless.
- **Why it's sticky:** call-and-response pulls the listener in to shout the answer back. It's ready-made for crowds and stadiums, and for people filming themselves doing it.

---

## Recommendation
**Concept A, "Skip-a-lo".** It's the purest earworm. It turns the brand name into a nonsense word that spreads to other words, and "lost-a-lo… found-a-lo" carries the insight without any explanation.
