# Aeralo Ad Jingle — Inspiration References

Brand: **Aeralo** (A-E-R-A-L-O)

These are the two reference jingles the concept and script should draw from.

## 1. Idea Cellular — "Honey Bunny"

What makes it work:
- **A silly, affectionate pet-name hook** ("Honey Bunny") repeated until it sticks.
- **Earworm simplicity:** a short, sing-song melody that's easy to hum, whistle or sing back.
- **Ringtone-able:** it worked as a standalone tune people played and sang, not just as an ad.
- **Everyday, playful tone:** light, a bit goofy, warm. It doesn't take itself seriously.
- **Spread by people:** it was widely hummed, shared and parodied in India.

Note: these are the hook mechanics. The ad's exact scenario and storyline still need the user to confirm.

## 2. Close-Up Toothpaste — "Kya aap Close-Up karte hain?"

Full line: *"Kya aap Close-Up karte hain, ya duniya se darte hain?"*

What makes it work:
- **The distinctive nasal, sing-song vocal:** this is its signature and the part people imitate.
- **A question as the hook:** it speaks directly to the listener ("Do you use Close-Up?").
- **A choice with a dare built in:** use the brand, or stay afraid of the world. Confidence versus hesitation.
- **The brand name is in the hook itself**, so you can't sing it without saying "Close-Up".
- **A rhyming couplet** (*karte hain / darte hain*) that's easy to remember.

## Takeaways for Aeralo

- Put **"Aeralo"** inside the hook itself, the way Close-Up does. Its open vowels (Ae-ra-lo) are easy to sing and stretch.
- Combine the **playful, hummable, pet-name-style repetition** of Honey Bunny with Close-Up's **nasal, sing-song question-and-dare delivery**.
- Aim for a short couplet or chant that works as a ringtone or reel audio on its own.
- Open questions: What category is Aeralo in? Who is the target audience? What language mix (Hindi, English or Hinglish)? How long is the spot (15s, 30s)?
